Job Market Paper

A Structural Gravity Analysis of Trade Under Imperfect Competition: Evidence from the Global Tree-Nut Market

Umesh Gautam and Stephen Devadoss

Global tree-nut markets are concentrated among a relatively small number of major suppliers, creating scope for oligopoly power in international trade. This paper incorporates market power into a structural gravity model, assuming a single firm in each country with multiproduct ownership. It evaluates three forms of market conduct—perfect competition, Bertrand competition, and Cournot competition—by comparing model-implied trade shares with observed trade shares. Free-trade counterfactuals then compare trade reallocation and welfare changes across conduct assumptions, with robustness exercises allowing more than one firm per country.

Current result The Cournot model currently provides the best fit to observed trade shares. The counterfactual results show that predicted trade and welfare responses to tariff removal differ meaningfully across assumptions about market conduct.

Implications of Mass Deportation for US Vegetable Production

Stephen Devadoss and Umesh Gautam (Authorship is shared equally.)

Journal of Agricultural and Resource Economics 51(3): 537–553.

Published ↗

We develop a theoretical model to analyze the impacts of deporting undocumented workers and implement it empirically for US vegetable production, which employs low-skilled domestic, guest, and undocumented workers, along with high-skilled workers, materials, and capital. The results show that removing 1,000 undocumented workers leads to a negligible increase in low-skilled domestic employment (59 workers) and an annual wage increase of $57 per worker, indicating very limited but positive labor-market adjustment. In contrast, reduced labor availability lowers vegetable output by $35.07 million and reduces returns to complementary inputs. Moreover, removing half of the undocumented workforce reduces vegetable output by $2.55 billion.
